# Baltic Drive — Apocalypse Story Concepts

**Status:** Story brainstorming / alternate lore pitches  
**Canon:** None selected yet  
**Setting:** Baltic Drive — a survival sandbox set in a real-world-based Poland / Baltic region  
**Core constraint:** No zombies. The threat comes from collapse, scarcity, human conflict, the environment, and (in some variants) mysteries that may never be fully explained.

> **Design principle:** The world should feel as though people once lived ordinary lives in it. The player discovers what happened through places, objects, vehicles, records, broadcasts, and survivors, rather than being forced through a linear exposition-heavy main quest.

---

## At a glance

| # | Concept | Type / tone | Central question |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Eleven Days** | Technological collapse; grounded slow-burn mystery | Why did the power fail, and who is turning parts of the city back on? |
| 2 | **The Evacuation** | Government mystery; conspiracy; exploration | Where did all the evacuated people go? |
| 3 | **Black Rain** | Environmental disaster; survival; atmospheric horror | What poisoned the rain, and why were some places spared? |
| 4 | **The Last Summer** | Social and economic collapse; human conflict | How do ordinary people survive after civilization stops functioning? |
| 5 | **The Signal** | Science-fiction mystery; psychological unease | What is broadcasting the tones, and what do the coordinates mean? |
| 6 | **The Other Side** | Regional catastrophe; isolation; uncertain outside world | Did the world end, or did only our region disappear from it? |

These are **six alternative foundations**, not six events that all have to happen. Elements can be combined deliberately if a coherent shared history emerges.

## 1. The Eleven Days

**Genre / atmosphere:** Technological collapse, realism, slow-burn mystery.  
**Pitch:** Civilization is dismantled in less than two weeks by cascading infrastructure failures.

### Story

It began on a Tuesday.

At **09:17**, the Polish electrical grid failed. Within minutes, neighboring countries experienced similar failures.

Nobody was particularly worried. Power outages happened.

By Wednesday, mobile networks were down. By Thursday, fuel deliveries had stopped. By Friday, supermarkets had been emptied.

On the seventh day, the military began evacuating major population centers.

On the eleventh day, the last national radio broadcast ended mid-sentence.

Nobody knows what caused the initial failure.

The strange thing?

**Somewhere, every night, entire sections of Warsaw briefly light up again.**

But nobody is living there.

### What players discover

- Evidence of routines abruptly interrupted: meals, work, school, road trips.
- Broken infrastructure and remnants of attempts to keep it operating.
- Records, warnings, and the final broadcast from the first eleven days.
- Districts that periodically regain electricity, without a clear explanation.

### Gameplay hooks

- Generators, fuel shortages, batteries, electrical systems, and valuable powered locations.
- Abandoned evacuation convoys and road blockages.
- Searches for supplies before a location or system becomes unreachable.
- Exploration motivated by mysteriously functioning infrastructure.

### Questions to leave open

- Was the original failure accidental, deliberate, or something nobody understood?
- Who—or what—is restoring power in parts of Warsaw?
- Do other cities experience the same phenomenon?

## 2. The Evacuation

**Genre / atmosphere:** Government mystery, conspiracy, exploration.  
**Pitch:** The population was ordered to leave, and most of those who obeyed were never seen again.

### Story

At **04:32**, every television, radio station, and mobile phone in Poland transmitted the same emergency message.

Citizens were instructed to evacuate immediately.

There was no explanation. Only designated routes and collection points.

Millions obeyed.

Trains departed full. Military buses carried entire neighborhoods away. Motorways became enormous rivers of vehicles.

But something went wrong.

Some convoys never reached their destinations. Others returned completely empty.

Three months later, the cities remain largely deserted.

A few survivors have returned to their homes. Others live by scavenging the abandoned infrastructure.

And the question remains:

**Where did everybody go?**

### What players discover

- Emergency announcements and evacuation maps.
- Passenger manifests, transport records, and hastily assembled reception centers.
- Vehicles left behind on designated evacuation routes.
- Testimonies from people who stayed, escaped, or returned.
- Conflicting explanations for the missing convoys.

### Gameplay hooks

- Follow real roads and rail corridors to reconstruct evacuation routes.
- Search transport hubs, schools, assembly points, and roadside camps.
- Track missing friends or relatives as optional personal stories.
- Discover why specific routes failed or ended abruptly.

### Questions to leave open

- Who issued the order, and on what information?
- What happened to the missing passengers?
- Were the evacuation centers ever safe?

## 3. Black Rain

**Genre / atmosphere:** Environmental catastrophe, survival, atmospheric horror.  
**Pitch:** An unexplained contamination turns rain and freshwater into threats, forcing humanity to reorganize around clean resources.

### Story

The first reports came from northern Europe.

Fish died in enormous numbers. Birds abandoned their migration routes. Strange chemical odors drifted over coastal cities.

Then came the rain.

It wasn't black. That was simply what people called it.

Because wherever it fell, something eventually died.

Authorities initially blamed industrial contamination. Then an atmospheric accident.

But the contamination kept spreading.

By the time officials understood that the water supply was compromised, entire regions were uninhabitable.

Years later, the rain has become less frequent. Some rivers are clean again. Forests have reclaimed abandoned towns.

But survivors still collect rainwater with extraordinary caution.

**And nobody understands why certain places were never affected.**

### What players discover

- Closed wells, warning notices, deserted water-treatment sites.
- Improvised filtration systems and abandoned attempts to protect drinking water.
- Contradictory records of rain patterns and supposedly safe areas.
- Isolated communities that survived by controlling a clean water source.

### Gameplay hooks

- Water purification and the need to distinguish safe from risky sources.
- Protective equipment and environmental exposure as survival systems.
- Contaminated locations containing valuable supplies.
- Settlements, trade, and conflict organized around clean wells or reservoirs.

### Questions to leave open

- What made the rain dangerous?
- Why were some areas unaffected?
- Is the danger actually fading or merely changing?

## 4. The Last Summer

**Genre / atmosphere:** Grounded social collapse, human conflict, melancholy survival.  
**Pitch:** There is no single apocalypse—only a long failure of interconnected systems.

### Story

There was no explosion.

No virus.

No mysterious signal.

The world simply ran out of ways to keep itself functioning.

An international financial crisis caused fuel shortages. Food transportation faltered. Factories stopped producing critical components.

Governments tried to stabilize the situation, but every emergency measure created another problem.

People began leaving cities.

The countryside seemed safer until agricultural machinery stopped running and fertilizer supplies disappeared.

The first winter killed more people than the preceding months of unrest.

By the following summer, the central government no longer controlled much of the country.

Five years later, small communities occupy the ruins of modern civilization.

Some trade.

Some farm.

Some rob travelers.

And most people just want to live another day.

**The world didn't end. Civilization did.**

### What players discover

- Gradual changes: shuttered businesses, makeshift markets, improvised repairs, deserted farms.
- Notes, diaries, and community records chronicling a drawn-out decline.
- Evidence of mutual aid as well as greed, fear, and violence.
- Places repurposed for agriculture, workshops, or shelter.

### Gameplay hooks

- Farming, water collection, crafting, and settlement building.
- Repairing and maintaining vehicles with scarce parts and fuel.
- Trade routes and a local economy in which almost everything has practical value.
- Bandit groups alongside neutral, cooperative, or hostile communities.
- Long-term survival without supernatural enemies or a required mystery.

### Questions to leave open

- Can people rebuild regional networks of trust and trade?
- Who controls fuel, food, tools, and safe passage?
- Is the collapse permanent, or simply a different era beginning?

## 5. The Signal

**Genre / atmosphere:** Science-fiction mystery, psychological horror, exploration.  
**Pitch:** A strange radio transmission precedes infrastructure failures and unexplained disappearances.

### Story

On **September 19**, every radio receiver in Europe began broadcasting an unusual signal.

It lasted exactly **43 seconds**.

There was no speech. No music.

Just a repeating sequence of tones.

Within hours, electricity began failing. Aircraft lost communication. Navigation systems behaved erratically.

Some people reported hearing the signal even when their radios were switched off.

Then came the disappearances.

Not entire cities. Just individuals.

A truck abandoned on a motorway with its engine running.

A family dinner left untouched.

A police patrol that never returned.

Eventually, society collapsed under the weight of fear and infrastructure failure.

Now, years later, the signal occasionally returns.

And survivors have noticed something unsettling.

**Every time it broadcasts, the same coordinates appear on old navigation devices.**

### What players discover

- Fragmentary recordings, technical logs, and firsthand accounts of the transmission.
- Vehicles and homes abandoned without clear evidence of violence.
- Radio equipment that behaves strangely under certain conditions.
- References to coordinates that may lead to a significant location.

### Gameplay hooks

- Radio scanning, collecting recordings, and interpreting clues.
- Searching transmitters, technical facilities, and isolated installations.
- Exploring GPS coordinates or following reports from survivors.
- Environmental unease without needing monsters or zombies.

### Questions to leave open

- Who—or what—is broadcasting the signal?
- Are the disappearances genuinely connected?
- What lies at the coordinates, and is reaching it a good idea?

## 6. The Other Side

**Genre / atmosphere:** Regional catastrophe, isolation, political thriller, uncertainty.  
**Pitch:** The Baltic region is cut off, and its survivors cannot tell whether the rest of the world is gone or simply unreachable.

### Story

Nobody knows exactly when the borders closed.

At first, governments described the crisis as a temporary security situation affecting the Baltic region.

Transport was suspended. Flights were grounded. International communications were restricted.

Entire populations were encouraged to leave.

Then the information stopped.

The survivors assumed the rest of the world had suffered the same collapse.

Years passed.

New communities formed. Bandits occupied highways. Old cities slowly decayed.

But occasionally, powerful radio transmitters pick up voices.

Not recordings.

**Live conversations.**

Commercial shipping traffic. Weather reports. Ordinary people discussing ordinary things.

Somewhere beyond the Baltic Sea, civilization might still exist.

And perhaps the most frightening possibility isn't that the entire world ended.

**It's that the world continued without them.**

### What players discover

- Closed crossings, abandoned checkpoints, and restrictions nobody fully explains.
- Military and civilian attempts to travel into or out of the region.
- Radio chatter that may be genuine, intercepted, or intentionally misleading.
- Signs of possible trade or contact outside the isolated zone.

### Gameplay hooks

- Exploration of coastal roads, ports, and maritime facilities.
- Boats and navigation if those systems become part of Baltic Drive.
- Radio interception and distant signals.
- Restricted military zones and hard-to-reach caches.
- A mystery that can expand naturally as the playable geography grows.

### Questions to leave open

- Is the region quarantined, abandoned, occupied, or deliberately isolated?
- Are the distant broadcasts real?
- Why has nobody come back for the survivors?

# Recommended Combination: The Eleven Days + The Evacuation

**Working recommendation, not canon.**

Of the six pitches, **The Eleven Days** and **The Evacuation** make a particularly strong combined foundation for an open-world survival sandbox.

### Combined premise

A cascading power failure spreads across Poland and neighboring countries. Communication and supply chains rapidly fail. Authorities order mass evacuations as the crisis intensifies. Millions leave cities such as Warsaw, but some convoys vanish or never reach their destination. Over time, the remaining inhabitants survive by scavenging, trading, and sometimes preying on each other.

Much later, the player explores this enormous, mostly empty world. At night, certain unoccupied Warsaw districts briefly regain electricity. Nobody knows why. Records suggest the evacuations were not as straightforward as survivors believe.

### Why it suits Baltic Drive

- **Real geography matters:** Roads, rail lines, stations, suburbs, and highways tell the story of evacuation attempts.
- **Existing survival systems fit:** Vehicles, fuel, loot, food, water, bandits, and parked cars naturally belong to this world.
- **Exploration is the narrative:** A roadblock or abandoned bus can be meaningful without a scripted cutscene.
- **Mystery is optional:** Players who only want to drive, build, and survive can do so; curious players can pursue clues.
- **The answer can remain ambiguous:** Different survivors can tell different, sometimes contradictory, versions of events.

### Possible evidence scattered across the world

- A radio recording from the early days of the crisis.
- A handwritten note on a refrigerator.
- A school turned into an emergency shelter.
- An abandoned military convoy on the road outside Radom.
- A family car packed for evacuation and left on a motorway.
- A former reception center with passenger lists but no trace of its occupants.
- A partially functioning electrical substation.
- A radio station, hundreds of kilometers away, still playing music from before the collapse.

None of these clues should require a large quest marker. They can reward curiosity and careful exploration.

### Open mysteries

1. What caused the initial cascading failures?
2. Who issued the evacuation order, and why?
3. Where did the missing convoys go?
4. Why do parts of Warsaw illuminate at night?
5. What does the outside world look like now?

These questions do not all need definitive answers.

# Optional Opening: The Last Ordinary Tuesday

**Alternative start-of-game idea, not a required tutorial or established feature.**

The player begins **before the apocalypse**, living an ordinary life in Warsaw.

It's a normal Tuesday morning. They get into a car, turn on the radio, and drive to work. Traffic moves. Shops are open. People wait at crossings. Nothing announces a grand disaster.

Then the traffic lights go out.

At first, it seems like a minor outage. Drivers honk. A bus blocks an intersection. The radio announcer begins reporting electrical problems. The player can choose how to react as the city gradually changes around them.

The ordinary environment becomes the first chapter of the catastrophe. Later, revisiting the same streets has an emotional impact because the player remembers them alive.

### What this opening could establish

- A personal connection to a specific neighborhood and vehicle.
- Basic driving and world interactions through ordinary activities.
- The contrast between the living city and its abandoned future.
- The feeling that the disaster is unfolding rather than simply being described.

### Important production consideration

A populated, functioning pre-collapse Warsaw would require substantial civilian simulation, traffic, audio, and event scripting. It is a **high-cost optional concept**, not a prerequisite for using the Eleven Days lore. A simpler implementation could communicate the same feeling with a short sequence in one small area.

# Shared Narrative Design Principles

These principles apply to any of the six apocalypse concepts.

## 1. No zombies

The threats are human conflict, hunger, thirst, bad weather, broken equipment, scarcity, and environmental danger. Strange phenomena, where used, can remain mysterious without becoming monster combat.

## 2. The sandbox comes first

Do not make a mandatory main campaign the only way to understand or enjoy the world. Players should be free to explore Poland, travel, repair cars, scavenge, build, trade, and survive at their own pace.

## 3. Tell stories through the map

A world based on real roads, settlements, forests, and cities has a natural storytelling advantage. Emergency shelters belong in plausible buildings; evacuation convoys belong on roads; supply caches belong in locations people would have chosen during the crisis.

## 4. Leave room for conflicting testimony

Survivors do not have to agree about what happened. Rumors, imperfect records, propaganda, misunderstandings, and personal memories can coexist. A convincing post-collapse world need not contain a single narrator who knows everything.

## 5. Ordinary objects can be the strongest clues

A note, a toy, an empty refrigerator, an untouched dinner, a family photograph, or a car loaded for a journey can be more affecting than an enormous exposition dump.

## 6. Let mechanics reinforce the fiction

Fuel, vehicle wear, batteries, looting, bandits, water, shelter, farming, and trading should arise naturally from the world's history rather than feel like unrelated survival meters.

## 7. Keep the geography expandable

Baltic Drive may grow beyond Warsaw or Poland. The lore should still function when the player reaches new towns, borders, coasts, and eventually other countries. Local evidence can expand the mystery without requiring the entire narrative to be rewritten.

# Decision Notes / What Has Not Been Decided

- **No apocalypse explanation has been selected as official canon.**
- The six concepts are intentionally alternatives; combining every element risks making the setting feel overcomplicated.
- The combined **Eleven Days + Evacuation** storyline is a suggested direction only.
- The playable pre-apocalypse Tuesday is optional and potentially expensive to implement.
- Exact dates, years, the time elapsed since the collapse, factions, named protagonists, and any definitive explanation of the mysteries remain open.
- Any supernatural or science-fiction component is optional; **The Last Summer** supports a completely grounded approach.

## One-sentence thematic pitch

> **Explore an immense, recognizable world after its systems have failed, and piece together what happened through the traces left by the people who once lived there.**
